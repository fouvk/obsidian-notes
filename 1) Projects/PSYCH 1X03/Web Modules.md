# Research Methods 1 (01)
scientific method ensures reliable and meaningful research:
1. construct theory
2. genereate hypothesis
3. choose research method
4. collect data
5. analyze data
6. report findings
7. revise existing theories
good scientists are always skeptical
## anecdotal evidence
- <u>evidence gathered by own or others</u>
- insufficient to draw scientific conclusions
	1. single exp. might not be representative
- testing hypothesis with experiments allows casual conclusions
- Independant variable changed, dependant variable is watched and measured
## groups in experiments
1. experimental group
	- uses manipulated IV
2. control group
	- does not used manupylated IV
- both should be as similar as possible
- compare afterwards to analyze
## within-participants design
- manipulating IV within each participant to minimize effect of participant differences on DV
	- i.e. repeatedly testing with 1 participant
- problem would be that time and change can be inideal
## confounding variable
- a var associated with IV obscuring effects on outcome, difficult to draw conclusions
	- selecting samples can limit confounding variables
	- strict selection critiera can hinder generalizability and scope of conclusions
## population
- all students the study is meant for
## sample
- subset chosen to represent population accurately
### random sample
- samples selected at random, no bias
	- random assignment: assigned to experimental/control group randomly as well
## placebo effect
- situation where a response to treatment is not from its real effects
## participant bias
- participants can have their own intentions
## experimenter bias
- actions made by experimenter effect the outcome
## blinding participants
- hide certain info of the expermient to participants/experimenters help eliminate placebo effect
- both: double blind
# Research Methods 2 (01)
cont. of scientific method
## descriptive statistics
- provides info of collected data at an overall glance
	- mean median mode
	- measures of central tendancies do not sufficiently summarize data
- charts graphs histograms visually used
- standard deviation
	- low - small spread
	- large - large spread
## inferential statistics
- stats that allow use from sample results to make population inferences
## T test
- statistical test considering each data point from both groups to calculate probability that 2 samples were drawn from same pop.
- differences of over 5% between tests concludes that there is in fact a difference between the groups, therefore cannot conclude correct
	- statistical significance: actual difference and not random
## errors
- type 1 error: difference believed, no actual difference, <u>false alarm</u>
- type 2 error: difference unseen, actual difference, <u>miss</u>
## correlation coefficient (r)
- between -1 and 1, strength and direction of correlation
	- values -1 and 1 mean the data is perfectly correlated with a consistent pattern
	- abs. magnitude between 0 and 1 indicates the strength of the pattern 
- correlation not equal causation
